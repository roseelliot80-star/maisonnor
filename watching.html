Start backyard birdwatching

You can start with a view from a window or a comfortable chair outside. Try watching the same spot for ten minutes on two or three different mornings.

Notice size and shape

Compare the bird to one you already know. Is it sparrow-sized or closer to a robin? Look for a crest, a pointed tail, or a short, thick bill. The bill and tail often give you a clue when color is hard to see.

Find one strong color clue

Notice where the color is. A rusty chest on an upright lawn visitor points toward an American Robin. Rusty sides on a bird scratching under shrubs can point toward a Spotted Towhee in much of the West.

Watch what the bird does

A robin often runs and pauses on the lawn. A Mourning Dove may walk slowly under a feeder. Note whether the bird visits the ground, a fence, a shrub, or the tree canopy.

Keep a simple record

Write down the date, place, and two clues before opening the photo guide. Use the printable checklist to keep sightings from different days together.
